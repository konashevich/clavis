Definition

What CLAVIS is

A cross-platform personal data vault and protocol — not a central identity database.

Holder vault

Stores passports, medical, financial, education, property, and residential credentials encrypted at rest on the user’s device.

Hardware gate

Signing and decryption keys live in a hardware security module. No HSM → out of scope for the protocol.

Consented release

Data leaves only as an encrypted Verifiable Presentation after biometrics / PIN (and step-up MFA when risk is high).

Section 01

The problem is structural, not a missing form field

Form fatigue, admin burden, data-entry harm, portal honeypots, and purpose-unbound check-ins.

Australia-first framing

Form fatigue & admin burden

People re-enter the same personal data across paper and digital forms — then organisations re-verify and repair errors.

  • Repeated identity, address, insurance, and history capture across healthcare, finance, hospitality, and government.
  • Manual verification and correction consume skilled labour that should be clinical or analytical work.
  • Admin friction compounds into a national productivity drag — not just user annoyance.

What CLAVIS changes

Replace “type it again” with a hardware-gated presentation of already-attested credentials — once consented, fields arrive in the verifier’s native schema.

Figures cited in early notes (e.g. healthcare error costs) are research placeholders from the design chat — treat as illustrative until owner-validated sources are locked.

High-stakes domain

Healthcare: errors at the front door

Wrong allergies, medications, or identifiers at admission are not paperwork problems — they are clinical risk.

Transcription risk

Hand-copied or re-keyed fields introduce silent corruption into the chart.

Stale copies

Portal PDFs and prior admissions may not reflect the issuer’s current attestation.

CLAVIS response

Issuer-signed semantic credentials (e.g. FHIR-shaped) presented selectively under patient control.

Rental & portals

Document honeypots

“Upload your passport PDF” creates concentrated, long-lived copies — even for unsuccessful applications.

Today

  • Millions of IDs sit on a few portals
  • Agencies retain copies for years
  • Breach surface is the whole archive
  • Applicant loses practical control

With CLAVIS

  • Credentials remain on the holder device
  • Verifier gets a purpose-bound presentation
  • Proofs (e.g. income threshold) replace bulk PDFs
  • Retention contract + deletion certificate

Structural privacy

Pandemic check-ins: the lesson

Emergency QR systems collected identity without purpose binding — trust eroded when data was reused beyond health.

Surveillance creep

Check-in data accessed for non-health investigations in several Australian jurisdictions — public trust damage.

Third-party sprawl

Unverified venue apps created fragmented honeypots of names and phones.

Design fix

Cryptographic purpose binding, ephemeral handshakes, and verification-over-collection (e.g. status tick without storing identity).

Section 03

Cross-platform by design. Hardware is non-negotiable.

Early Android-only framing was a draft mistake. The protocol is platform-independent.

Critical correction

Platform stance

Target: Android, iOS, Linux, Windows (and equivalents). Devices without hardware-backed crypto are out of scope.

Android Keystore / StrongBox
iOS Secure Enclave
Linux TEE / TPM path
Windows TPM / platform crypto

APIs differ; the protocol does not. Canvas titles like “Secure Android Data Vault” are historical draft language — not product scope.

Hard principles

Security model

Keys in HSM

Signing / unwrap keys are non-exportable. Identity presentations cannot be cloned to non-TEE devices.

Encrypted at rest

Credentials never sit as plaintext on disk. Decrypt only for an authorised presentation, then wipe memory.

No mass export

Protocol forbids bulk plaintext dump. Release is per-request, encrypted to the verifier’s key.

Local audit

Immutable receipt of demand: who, what, when, purpose, retention — held by the holder.

Subject authorization

The hardware gate

The HSM releases key use only after successful user verification — biometrics and/or PIN.

Baseline

  • Show verifier identity + trust tier
  • Show requested fields + purpose + retention
  • User verification unlocks signing / decrypt

Step-up MFA

  • Triggered for higher-risk transfers
  • Especially to Registered (not Trusted) operators
  • e.g. biometric + PIN before medical / financial release

In-person assurance

Face-to-data binding

A phone can be handed over. A photo bound into the credential tree makes physical impersonation harder.

  • Portrait is part of the Verifiable Credential / Merkle structure — not a loose JPEG.
  • Verifier receives fields + photo + proof; first validates crypto, then visually matches the person.
  • PoC nuance: tree may hold a high-res hash while a thumbnail travels for fast display.

Human factor

CLAVIS protects the identification act itself: the face on the verifier screen is cryptographically tethered to the attested fields.

Section 04

No honeypot database. Speak the verifier’s language.

Index + containers. Industry schemas. Selective disclosure.

Encrypted Document Store

Index + per-document containers

Not one relational dump of every passport and lab result.

Encrypted index

UI metadata only: doc id, type, title, issue date. No PII payload. Unlocked to paint the home screen.

Platform example: encrypted preferences / secure store — not a product lock-in.

Document containers

BLOBPassport VC
BLOBFHIR Patient
BLOBIncome attest.
BLOBLease attest.

Lazy decrypt one container at a time → present → wipe RAM. Deleting a file is Right-to-be-Forgotten at the filesystem.

Architecture thesis

There is no central user database

No single dump

Compromise of one container does not yield the whole archive.

Native VCs

Store original JSON-LD — avoid remapping passport fields into SQL tables.

Deletion that works

Physical file removal beats scrubbing indexes across a monolithic DB.

Zero-mapping intent

Standards over proprietary fields

CLAVIS speaks Schema.org, FHIR, CDR / ISO 20022 — so verifiers do not invent a “CLAVIS dialect.”

  • W3C DID / VC — trust triangle, presentations, proofs.
  • HL7 FHIR — medical resources hospitals already parse.
  • CDR / ISO 20022 — finance-shaped payloads.
  • Open Badges / ELM — education credentials.

Zero-mapping

Verifier engineers use standard libraries. familyName means the same thing in the vault and in their system.

New domains add a @context — not a fork of the vault architecture.

Illustrative shape

Semantic credential (medical sketch)

Working example for specialists — not a locked schema.

  • @context binds W3C VC + FHIR vocabulary.
  • DIDs identify issuer and subject.
  • Proof can anchor a Merkle root so redacted leaves remain verifiable.

Data minimisation

Selective disclosure (Merkle)

Issuer signs the root. Holder reveals only needed leaves — with proofs for the rest.

  • One full encrypted document; many possible presentations.
  • Car rental needs name + licence entitlement — not home address or medical restrictions.
  • Aligns with GDPR / Australian Privacy Principles minimisation.
Merkle Root (issuer-signed)
Name — revealed
Licence class — revealed
Home address — withheld
Medical notes — withheld

Roadmap · speculative depth

Beyond Merkle: predicate proofs

Merkle still reveals the unredacted raw value. True “over 18” without DOB needs ZKP / BBS+-class signatures.

Near-term PoC

Merkle selective disclosure is enough to prove the privacy granularity thesis.

Later upgrade path

Architecture should not block BBS+ / ZKP later — unlinkability and predicates are desirable, not PoC-critical. Marked speculative until chosen.

Contextual authorisation

Domain–data affinity & anomaly flags

Affinity check

Compare verifier domain / declared scope to requested credential categories before showing a calm consent screen.

Anomaly flag

Cross-domain overreach lights up in the UI and can force step-up MFA — even if the DID signature verifies.

Lifecycle contract

Retention, purpose, deletion

Every presentation request carries signed retention metadata — not a vague privacy policy link.

FieldMeaning
Retention periodEphemeral, limited window, or statutory retention
Purpose bindingCryptographic commitment to stated use only (e.g. “health check-in”)
Certificate of DeletionOn expiry, verifier returns a signed deletion proof to the holder
Revocation requestHolder can demand erasure; verifier must confirm or cite legal hold
Registry consequenceMissing deletion certificates → retention violation / trust degradation

Protocol phases

Logical dataflow

Phase I
Request

Verifier sends DID, required fields, retention policy. Holder verifies DID + domain–data affinity.

Phase II
Authorize

UI shows trust status. HSM unlocks after biometric / MFA. Step-up for high-risk Registered operators.

Phase III
Present

Hardware-signed VP encrypted to verifier public key. Local immutable audit: who / what / when / expiry.

Mutual accountability

Signed dynamic requests

The verifier does not “ask casually” — it issues a signed obligation. Non-repudiation for both sides.

  • Organisation long-term cert + per-transaction signature.
  • Dynamic JWT/JWS fields: iat, exp, nonce, aud.
  • Stops replay / phishing reuse of stale request QR links.
  • Holder keeps cryptographic receipt of demand.

Why it matters

After a leak, the citizen can prove Clinic X requested fields A & B for purpose C on date D — and Clinic X cannot claim the user volunteered them unsolicited.

Adoption ladder

Dynamic vs static manifests

High-stakes verifiers get short-lived signed requests. Low-stakes sites can host a one-time signed well-known file.

Dynamic (high-stakes)

Banks, clinics, government. SDK mints per-session JWT. UI: protected session.

Static (low-stakes)

/.well-known/clavis/manifest.json signed once. Nickname / age-over-18 class fields. UI: basic verification. Replay risk → hard-limit away from sensitive PII.

Section 06

Web handshake and proximity exchange

Compliant sites, legacy form-fill fallback, QR / NFC / BLE, offline ISO 18013-5 style.

Browser / extension path

Compliant handshake vs legacy fill

Compliant

Site embeds schema metadata. Extension / client detects it and runs an encrypted vault handshake — full protocol path.

Legacy fallback

Heuristic field mapping + proactive overlay for user confirm. Inject via HTTPS with explicit warning: no encrypted CLAVIS handshake.

In-person channels

QR · NFC · BLE

Hospital desks, agencies, and banks need more than a browser.

QR

Verifier shows session QR; holder scans → secure session. Works with a screen or paper stand.

NFC

Tap-to-verify: centimetre-range engagement, pay-like UX, strong physical presence.

BLE

Larger payloads (e.g. imaging-heavy records) over a short-lived local link when online is weak.

Offline-capable

ISO 18013-5 style engagement

Two stages: device engagement, then encrypted peer data retrieval — even without cellular.

Engage

NFC tap or QR

Exchange channel parameters; QR may bootstrap an encrypted BLE session.

Authorize

Biometric gate

User confirms verifier + fields on-device before any payload leaves.

Retrieve

P2P encrypted

Session keys die after the check. Basement wards and remote sites still work.

Smartphone as next-gen smart card — agencies upgrade terminals/software, not plastic inventory.

Detour · open design

Lost device & key recovery

Hardware-bound keys are a feature and a UX hazard. Recovery policy must not recreate exportable masters.

Tension

Non-exportable HSM keys mean “restore from cloud backup of private key” is incompatible with the security model.

Likely direction

Re-enrolment + re-issuance from Issuers; encrypted container backup that only unwraps on a new attested HSM after strong identity proof.

Status

Recovery UX remains an open protocol question from the design chat — not a locked CLAVIS claim yet.
