  1. Trust Gap: Consumer apps need "Remote Attestation" to prove the Vault is genuine.
  2. App Sandboxing: OS designs restrict Inter-Process Communication (IPC), making standardized data exchange difficult without a custom protocol.
  3. Walled Gardens: Platform holders (Google/Apple) prefer to act as the primary identity providers, limiting third-party "Identity Brokers" from accessing native biometric hooks for external apps.

4. Market Opportunity: The Universal Data Broker

The proposed specification describes a Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI) Agent. The unique opportunity lies in:

  • SVIAP Protocol: Standardizing how any app requests data securely.
  • Data Sovereignty: Moving beyond login credentials to allow users to own and share structured, encrypted personal data.
  • Privacy-by-Design: Ensuring that even the host OS cannot intercept the plaintext data during the exchange between the Vault and the Consumer.

# Market Review: Secure Data Vault Solutions

## 1. Current Landscape Overview
As of current market analysis, there is no single "universal" application that functions as a general-purpose, hardware-locked broker for third-party data requests. The market is fragmented into three primary categories, each implementing specific components of the proposed Secure Vault logic.

## 2. Competitive Analysis

### 2.1 Digital Identity Wallets (The "Protocol" Precursor)
* **Examples:** European Digital Identity Wallet (EUDI), Samsung Wallet, Apple Wallet IDs.
* **Storage Logic (Correction):** These apps do **not** store the bulk identity data *inside* the TEE.
    * **TEE/Secure Element:** Stores the **private cryptographic keys** and handles the biometric matching logic.
    * **Encrypted Storage:** The actual identity data (name, DOB, passport number) is stored in the app's private, encrypted database on the standard flash storage, but the **decryption key** is locked inside the TEE.
* **Gaps:** Restricted to official government-issued documents. They do not support user-defined "custom" records (e.g., private lease agreements or specific medical notes) for general third-party application use.

### 2.2 Hardware-Backed Password Managers (The "Storage" Precursor)
* **Examples:** Bitwarden, 1Password, SecureX.
* **Strengths:** Excellent use of HSMs to protect the "Master Key."
* **Gaps:** Primarily use "Push" technology (Autofill). They lack a secure bidirectional handshake protocol where data is re-encrypted specifically for a recipient's public key before transmission.

### 2.3 Platform Credential Managers (The "Auth" Precursor)
* **Examples:** Android Credential Manager, Apple Passkeys.
* **Strengths:** Uses hardware-backed keys to sign challenges for app logins.
* **Gaps:** Focus strictly on *authentication* (identity verification) rather than *data exchange* (sharing specific encrypted records).

## 3. The "Missing Link": Why it doesn't exist yet
Several barriers have prevented the emergence of a universal vault:
1.  **Trust Gap:** Consumer apps need "Remote Attestation" to prove the Vault is genuine.
2.  **App Sandboxing:** OS designs restrict Inter-Process Communication (IPC), making standardized data exchange difficult without a custom protocol.
3.  **Walled Gardens:** Platform holders (Google/Apple) prefer to act as the primary identity providers, limiting third-party "Identity Brokers" from accessing native biometric hooks for external apps.

## 4. Market Opportunity: The Universal Data Broker
The proposed specification describes a **Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI) Agent**. The unique opportunity lies in:
* **SVIAP Protocol:** Standardizing how any app requests data securely.
* **Data Sovereignty:** Moving beyond login credentials to allow users to own and share structured, encrypted personal data.
* **Privacy-by-Design:** Ensuring that even the host OS cannot intercept the plaintext data during the exchange between the Vault and the Consumer.
